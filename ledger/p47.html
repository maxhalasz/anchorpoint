the door

PHOTO: Maxa full page: the door sketched from three angles, plus a copy of the mark at eye height, plus a paratech compound label taped in

A maintenance door in a sub-level corridor, painted the same municipal green as the wall, no handle plate. I had walked past it every working day. The morning I finally saw it as a door there was a mark on it at eye height — the mark from p.44 — and I understood that nobody else in that building had ever seen the door at all.

It was held by an ordinary lock and by a working. The DRE filed this class of thing under one word. It does not make anything invisible. It reaches into the part of you that builds the picture and tells that part there is nothing there to build — so you fill the gap with wall, with pipework, with whatever is dull enough, and you walk on. Eyes will not settle on it. People lose the layout of a room, an object, a way in, and do not notice they have lost anything.

The division milked it. A liquid off it that scrubs your sense of direction. A gas off it, for cities — brush it on a doorway and the public will route around a building that is standing in plain sight.

I get through it because of what went into me when I was eight. Whatever the working leans on to do its work, mine is already broken in that exact place. It has nothing to push against.

The word for it is ████████. Behind it: paper, floor to ceiling.

The lock gives. The working does not resist me. It never has.
