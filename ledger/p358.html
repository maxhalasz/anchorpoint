A front-page article. Write it as a local paper would: short sentences, the fire, the number of children, where they were taken, an official saying the cause is under review, a neighbour quote. Leave the year vague or make it 1991.

Somewhere low in the column, one line that a normal reader skips: the children were placed with “a private welfare contractor” and not returned to county care.

Sally House. That is where they took me from, with the others. It burned the same year the DRE stopped answering. I do not think those two things are a coincidence and I have stopped pretending I do.

The key phrase is not sentimental. It is the name of the place, said the way you would say it to a child at the door of a dormitory. That is the whole trick of a good passphrase — make it something nobody would ever guess mattered.
