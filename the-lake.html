THE TOWN THAT EMPTIED IN A YEAR NOBODY WILL NUMBER

A lakeside settlement lost most of its people in the late seventies. The people who remain do not agree on when, and the county cannot say why. — BYLINE / BODY: Max
Main Street, Stillwater, 1974. Population then was near eight hundred.

Write this as a real regional paper would: a reporter drives out, finds a small town by a lake with maybe sixty residents, most of them elderly, and tries to establish what happened.

Key facts to fold in: the population fell from roughly eight hundred to under a hundred across about twelve months at the end of the 1970s; county records for that period are incomplete; and although a great many people are said to have left or gone missing in that window, the sheriff's office of the day recorded almost no missing-person reports — because, in the words of one retired deputy, no one came forward to say anyone was gone.

Include a resident quote that is vague and wrong-footed — someone who cannot picture the neighbours they say they had, who describes looking at an old class photograph and finding the faces "hard to hold." Include a line that the water in the lake has never given anything back.

Close on the reporter finding no proof of anything, only a town that does not want the year counted, and a note that the paper's own morgue is missing that year's editions.
