Welcome to AnchorPoint

If you have found your way here, chances are you are carrying something you feel you cannot hand to anyone else. I know that weight. I know how loud a quiet room gets after midnight, and how your own head can turn into the most hostile place you know.

For a long time I kept all of it to myself. I thought admitting what went on in my mind was a kind of surrender — proof I was weak. The truth is simpler. Nobody was built to carry the whole world alone. This page grew out of a stretch of personal ruin, and then out of a run of discoveries that changed my life.

You do not need every answer today. You do not need to fix all of it now. Sometimes the first step toward quiet is not fighting the noise, but learning to sit down and talk with your own head.

Make yourself at home. This place is yours.

— The Author

Find support

There is no account to make and nothing to sign. Write what you need to write. It does not have to be organized, and it does not have to make sense to anyone but you. People find comfort in putting a feeling into words, even when nothing is sent back.


 Every message is completely anonymous and untraceable.

Where the money goes

AnchorPoint costs about $60 a month to keep online. Anything past that pays for a second phone line in the evenings and for printing small cards with crisis numbers that I leave at two shelters across town. That is the whole budget. There is no office and no salary.

So far this month: $23 of $60. If you have already found your footing, $3 once covers a good part of a month. If you have not found it yet, keep your money and read on.

If it helps to know
  • In a given year, roughly one in five adults lives with a mental health condition.
  • Close to 60% of them will never talk to a professional about it.
  • Depression is among the leading causes of disability worldwide.
  • For people aged 15 to 29, suicide is one of the top causes of death.
  • The typical delay between first symptoms and first treatment is about a decade.
  • Most people improve with support — plain support, not necessarily treatment.
  • Reaching out once makes reaching out again easier.
